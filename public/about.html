About CCFDDL

CCFDDL is an open-source conference tracker for computer science researchers. It brings submission milestones, conference dates, locations and ranking labels together so you can compare venues and plan a research calendar.

What the tracker adds

A conference website describes one event. Planning a submission often means checking several conferences, multiple rounds and several time zones. CCFDDL organizes those details into a shared dataset and views built for that comparison:

  • Subject and CCF, CORE and TH-CPL filters help narrow a venue shortlist.
  • Separate abstract, paper, rebuttal and decision milestones help distinguish the next action from the conference dates.
  • Time-zone conversion and countdowns help translate a published cutoff into your working time zone.
  • Past editions and available acceptance-rate records provide context for planning. They do not predict the next edition's deadlines or your paper's chance of acceptance.
  • Favorites, calendar links and optional email reminders help keep a selected set of editions in view.

The interactive tracker, year comparison table and conference directory serve different reading needs. The directory also provides individual conference and edition pages with recorded milestones and links to the conference website.

Where the data comes from

The public conference files on GitHub contain the records used by the site. Each edition can include its official website, submission rounds, source time zone, event dates and location. Ranking fields record CCF, CORE and TH-CPL labels; a missing or non-listed label is not a judgment about a paper or research topic.

The project is maintained through community contributions and automation, including agent-assisted collection. Contribution guidance prioritizes the exact edition's official conference website, call for papers, important-dates page and linked submission system. An older edition or a third-party listing can help locate information, but does not establish a newly announced deadline.

How updates are checked

  1. A contributor proposes a change to the conference's YAML record through a public pull request. The proposal should identify official supporting information for the edition being changed.
  2. Repository checks validate the data's required fields and format, assemble the dataset and build the site. These checks catch structural problems; they cannot prove that a date agrees with an external website.
  3. Pull-request discussion and review provide a place to compare the proposed facts with their sources, resolve conflicting dates and request corrections before merging. The public change history remains available after an update.

Entries can lag behind an announcement or an extension, and source pages can change. The presence of a record is not a guarantee that every field has received a fresh human review. Check the official conference instructions before making a submission.

Published information, missing details and estimates

TBD means the record does not contain a confirmed date. When the interactive tracker shows an estimated next deadline, it labels the estimate and the historical edition it is based on. An estimate is a planning hint, not an announcement from the organizers.

Repository conventions can supply missing precision: when a source gives a date without a clock time, the convention is the end of that date; when it omits a time zone, the convention is AoE. These defaults are not statements made by the organizers. Read entry comments and the official source when the exact cutoff matters.

CCFDDL is an independent reference tool. Conference organizers control deadlines, tracks, eligibility and submission rules. Follow their current instructions if they differ from this site.

Report an error or contribute

For a wrong or missing deadline, open a GitHub issue with the conference name, edition, affected field and a direct link to the official evidence. Include the published time zone and the relevant round or track; these often explain apparent discrepancies.

You can also follow the contribution instructions and propose a correction. Existing discussions are available in the issue tracker and pull requests. Please keep personal or confidential information out of public reports.

Questions or corrections? Contact the maintainers on GitHub Issues.
