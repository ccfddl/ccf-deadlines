Plan a submission with CCFDDL

Start with a shortlist, check the correct edition and round, then translate the published cutoff into your time zone. Use reminders as a backup to that plan.

1. Build a conference shortlist

On the tracker, search for a conference's short name or edition ID, such as CVPR or cvpr27. Subject buttons narrow the results by research area. On small screens those buttons use short category codes, such as AI or DB.

CCF, CORE and TH-CPL are separate ranking filters. You can select multiple levels within a filter. Selecting CCF A and B matches either level; adding CORE A* further requires a matching CORE label. A filter with no selected levels imposes no restriction. If results disappear, check all three filters and the search field.

Non-CCF, Non-CORE and Non-THCPL include records marked non-listed or without a value for that system. Rankings are venue-level reference labels, not a guarantee of topical fit or acceptance. Read the conference's call for papers to decide whether its scope matches your work.

2. Read the next milestone, not just the year

The year in a conference name is the event edition. Its paper deadline may be in the previous calendar year. Conference dates and location describe attendance; they do not replace the submission cutoff.

Open a conference's details to inspect the recorded milestones and round comments:

Abstract submission
A separate abstract or registration cutoff, when recorded. Submitting a paper later may depend on completing this step.
Paper submission
The paper cutoff for the specified round. A conference can have several rounds or tracks with different dates.
Rebuttal and final decisions
Later review milestones, when available. The next countdown can advance to these after a paper deadline. Enable Show submission only to focus on abstract and paper cutoffs.
Conference opening
A countdown reference derived from the first listed conference date, using 08:00 in the record's submission time zone. It is not a verified local opening time. Check the organizer's program for attendance and travel planning.

TBD means a confirmed date is missing from the record. An estimated date is extrapolated from a previous edition's schedule and identifies that source year. Neither is a confirmed opportunity to submit. An estimate may become inaccurate if organizers change the season, submission model or number of rounds.

If a source states only a date, the repository convention uses the end of that date. If it omits the zone, the convention is AoE. Those defaults add usable precision to the record; they should not be mistaken for explicit organizer instructions. Check the official website and entry comments before relying on the last hour.

3. Check the source zone and your display zone

The time-zone selector beside the clock changes the display zone. The main tracker starts from your browser's time zone unless you have a saved preference; the table and directory can also use a time zone in their URL filters. The event still has one deadline instant: changing the display zone changes how that instant is shown, not how much time remains.

Common time-zone labels
LabelHow to read it
UTCThe zero-offset reference zone. A fixed label such as UTC-8 stays eight hours behind UTC throughout the year.
AoEAnywhere on Earth, represented as UTC-12. Its calendar day ends later than the same date in most local zones.
PTUS Pacific Time. The tracker applies US daylight saving dates: normally UTC-8 in winter and UTC-7 in summer. Check organizer instructions carefully around clock changes.
Asia/ShanghaiAn IANA time-zone name, used for mainland China's UTC+8 time. The standard identifier is Asia/Shanghai, including when you are in Beijing.
America/New_YorkA regional zone whose offset changes with daylight saving time. Use the deadline date's offset, rather than assuming today's offset applies all year.

For example, May 1 at 23:59 AoE is May 2 at 19:59 in Asia/Shanghai. It is still the same instant, although the calendar dates differ. Named display zones use the browser's time-zone rules for the event date. An offset shown beside a zone in email settings describes its current offset; it may differ in another season.

The main clock can also set a custom countdown base time. This previews the schedule relative to that chosen moment. Reset the base time when you want live countdowns again.

4. Compare editions without treating history as an announcement

Enable Show past conferences to bring completed editions back into the main list. This is useful for reviewing past submission patterns or finding an earlier event's official link. If available, acceptance-rate history supplies context about past editions; the coverage and reporting detail vary by conference.

The tabular view groups conference dates for year-to-year comparison and lets you switch between paper submission and conference opening dates. The directory provides subject, conference-series and individual-edition pages, including links to other recorded editions. Its text and conference links remain readable without loading the interactive tracker.

Historical timing can help you decide when to look for a new call for papers. It cannot confirm that a future edition will use the same schedule.

5. Save favorites and choose email reminders

Sign in with GitHub and click a conference's star to save that edition. Open My Favorites from the account menu to see your saved timeline. Stars refer to individual editions, so check and star the new edition when planning a new year.

Open Email Reminders in the account menu, select a time zone and English or Chinese, then authorize access to your verified primary GitHub email. This is separate from simply browsing deadlines. The dialog shows the destination address after reminders are enabled.

  • Standard reminders combine starred deadline notices into an email at 9 AM in the selected time zone, 7 days and 1 day before the deadline.
  • With Daily reminders enabled, the daily 9 AM email summarizes upcoming milestones from starred conferences.
  • Save changes to the time zone and language in that dialog. You can disable reminders there as well.

Email settings have their own time-zone choice; changing the tracker's clock does not save the email setting. Delivery can depend on the email service and your mailbox, so keep your own submission plan. The privacy policy explains the account and reminder data involved.

6. Choose a calendar import or a subscription

A conference's details offer calendar actions for its known milestones. A downloaded .ics file is a snapshot: importing it does not create an updating subscription.

For an updating calendar, use Subscribe in the main toolbar for category and ranking subscription links, or choose Batch Subscribe from the account menu to select starred editions. Batch subscriptions support up to 100 selected editions. The generated link contains that selected set; create a new link if you want a different set of editions.

Copy the subscription URL into your calendar application's subscribe-by-URL option. The batch dialog's Google Calendar action copies the URL and explains where to paste it; iCloud can open a webcal link. Calendar applications refresh subscriptions on their own schedules, so an extension announced on the website may take time to appear in your calendar.

Before submitting, visit the official conference website from the entry and verify the edition, round, track, clock time and zone. If you find a discrepancy, report it with the official source.
